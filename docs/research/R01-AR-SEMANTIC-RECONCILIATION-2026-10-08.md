# R01-A — Semantic comparison of Domain 13's short and expanded lenses

**Status: RESEARCH INTERPRETATION / NOT CANONICAL / NOT APPROVED.**
**Source:** `Branch · Research AI Writing Problems.md` (SRC-003), checked against the original 1-based file lines. The source is a chat export containing both a compact taxonomy and later expanded guidance. Both are preserved verbatim in the original source. No standalone fiction guidance archive has been independently verified.

## Research question and approach

The short master Domain 13 taxonomy (lines 2324–2354) and the expanded Domain 13 manual (lines 18081–19910) use ten matching identifiers (AR-01 through AR-10) but different titles. Are the meanings unchanged, overlapping, or different? Also review DS-09, PV-05 and PV-10, which exhibit other title drift.

The comparison examined each compact one- or two-sentence rule alongside the expanded lens's **Core principle**, **Common drift**, **Diagnostic questions**, **Better direction**, **Valid exceptions**, **Long-form warning signs** and **Revision considerations**, plus Domain 13 cross-lens guidance (from line 19174) and identity audit sections. The result below is a **proposed semantic crosswalk**, not an authorized replacement of either variant.

### Classification standard

- **Equivalent core:** same principal editorial question/problem; one version may provide more details and exceptions. *Does not mean byte-identical text.*
- **Overlapping:** meaningful common ground but one version emphasizes a distinct diagnostic dimension; preserve both rather than substituting one blindly.
- **Distinct:** materially different questions, evidence or editor behavior; sharing an ID would cause loss or misinterpretation if one overwrote the other.
- **Cannot determine:** missing or ambiguous source evidence; none of the 10 AR pairs falls here based on present text, but authority/supersession intent remains indeterminate.

## Executive assessment

Of **10 Domain 13 pairs**:
- Equivalent core: **3** (AR-01, AR-03, AR-08).
- Overlapping: **4** (AR-02, AR-05, AR-07, AR-10).
- Distinct: **3** (AR-04, AR-06, AR-09).

For the **3 additional title differences**:
- DS-09: equivalent core.
- PV-05: overlap with a broader expanded definition.
- PV-10: distinct mechanism despite nearly matching titles; earlier title-only review underestimated this difference.

This is a judgment based on source passages, **not a source statement**. No evidence was found in the inspected conversation that the author explicitly approved one list as the authoritative replacement for the other. The later expanded manual may reflect refinement, divergence, or an untracked rewrite; temporal sequence alone is insufficient to infer approval.

## Domain 13: individual semantic evidence

### AR-01 — Equivalent core

**Short index:** “Emergent theme” (SRC-003 lines 2326–2327).  
**Expanded manual:** “Theme through embodiment” (SRC-003 lines 18179–18288).

**Source-supported comparison:** The short instruction asks themes to arise from choices, consequences, patterns and contradictions. The expanded lens grounds theme in character actions, institutions, imagery and outcomes rather than repeated thematic explanation.

**Important difference / exceptions:** The expanded entry adds diagnosis and explicit exceptions for philosophical, religious, political and reflective fiction. Those exceptions should not be dropped.

**Proposed cross-reference, NOT a merge:** Same primary purpose; retain both phrasings as cited variants.

**Risk:** Low conceptual collision risk.

### AR-02 — Overlapping, not equivalent

**Short index:** “Thematic restraint” (SRC-003 lines 2329–2330).  
**Expanded manual:** “Thematic multiplicity” (SRC-003 lines 18289–18372).

**Source-supported comparison:** The short instruction resists explaining the lesson of every emotional event. The expanded lens instead resists forcing characters and consequences toward a single correct thematic answer, protecting serious counterarguments.

**Important difference / exceptions:** Unnecessary theme announcement and an over-neat thematic verdict are related but different failures. The short concern is discussed in expanded AR-01 and the cross-lens 'Theme announcement' section.

**Proposed cross-reference, NOT a merge:** Short thematic restraint → expanded AR-01 plus cross-lens Theme announcement; expanded AR-02 adds multiplicity/contradiction.

**Risk:** Medium: using one as the other would skip either explicitness or pluralistic argument.

### AR-03 — Equivalent core

**Short index:** “Symbolic accumulation” (SRC-003 lines 2332–2333).  
**Expanded manual:** “Symbolic restraint” (SRC-003 lines 18373–18454).

**Source-supported comparison:** The short instruction says symbols should gather meaning without repeated decoding. The expanded section states meaning emerges through recurrence, context, transformation and association, warns against decoding, and explicitly says 'Allow symbols to accumulate.'

**Important difference / exceptions:** The expanded framing foregrounds restraint rather than accumulation, but both are coupled in the text. It also distinguishes overt symbolism as a valid artistic exception.

**Proposed cross-reference, NOT a merge:** One underlying editorial concern, two useful labels.

**Risk:** Low if the full definitions and valid exceptions travel together.

### AR-04 — Distinct

**Short index:** “Moral complexity” (SRC-003 lines 2335–2336).  
**Expanded manual:** “Motif evolution” (SRC-003 lines 18455–18538).

**Source-supported comparison:** The short entry resists neat moral interpretation. The expanded entry tracks semantic change in repeated objects, images, phrases and situations across the manuscript.

**Important difference / exceptions:** Morality/interpretive ambiguity and historical transformation of motifs require different questions and evidence. The expanded manual covers moral neatness through AR-02 and the cross-lens 'Moral tidiness' section, while expanded AR-04 concerns motif history.

**Proposed cross-reference, NOT a merge:** Short moral complexity → expanded AR-02 and cross-lens Moral tidiness; expanded motif evolution → short AR-03 partially, but adds its own evolving-meaning check.

**Risk:** HIGH: treating the versions as the same AR-04 would misassign a narrative-checking rule.

### AR-05 — Overlapping, not identical

**Short index:** “Genre intelligence” (SRC-003 lines 2338–2339).  
**Expanded manual:** “Genre-promise awareness” (SRC-003 lines 18539–18629).

**Source-supported comparison:** The short instruction favors knowing use of conventions rather than genre averages. The expanded entry asks what emotional/dramatic experience the genre promises and distinguishes that from surface markers.

**Important difference / exceptions:** Genre intelligence is broader; genre reader-contract awareness is a focused component. The expanded version protects experimental and anti-genre exceptions.

**Proposed cross-reference, NOT a merge:** Expanded AR-05 elaborates a major subcase of the shorter AR-05; expanded AR-06 additionally handles trope-specific execution.

**Risk:** Low-to-medium: avoid reducing all genre awareness to mandatory genre beat sheets.

### AR-06 — Distinct

**Short index:** “Work-specific obsessions” (SRC-003 lines 2341–2342).  
**Expanded manual:** “Convention with specificity” (SRC-003 lines 18630–18717).

**Source-supported comparison:** The short entry asks a novel to develop recurring concerns belonging uniquely to it. The expanded entry asks how familiar plots and tropes become distinctive through specific execution rather than high-probability genre outcomes.

**Important difference / exceptions:** Distinctive concerns recurring within a work are not the same as specific treatment of a known trope. The short theme is closer to expanded AR-07, AR-10 and the project-level identity test.

**Proposed cross-reference, NOT a merge:** Short obsessions → expanded AR-07/AR-10 and identity profile; expanded convention specificity → short AR-05 genre intelligence (related but more specific).

**Risk:** HIGH: would overwrite a work-level identity lens with a genre-trope execution lens.

### AR-07 — Overlapping, not identical

**Short index:** “Authorial attention” (SRC-003 lines 2344–2345).  
**Expanded manual:** “Aesthetic coherence” (SRC-003 lines 18718–18820).

**Source-supported comparison:** Short entry addresses habitual selections of what a narrative notices, ignores and values. Expanded entry asks whether prose has a recognizable aesthetic center and recurring artistic priorities; its diagnostic questions explicitly include what the novel notices.

**Important difference / exceptions:** Selection of attention is a mechanism inside aesthetic coherence; aesthetic coherence also considers rhythm, imagery, stylistic modes and variation.

**Proposed cross-reference, NOT a merge:** Short authorial attention → a key component of expanded AR-07 and expanded AR-10.

**Risk:** Medium: retaining aesthetic coherence only may obscure the more precise attention criterion.

### AR-08 — Equivalent core

**Short index:** “Productive eccentricity” (SRC-003 lines 2347–2348).  
**Expanded manual:** “Idiosyncrasy preservation” (SRC-003 lines 18821–18914).

**Source-supported comparison:** Short entry protects strange but coherent choices that contribute identity. Expanded entry protects unusual but functional language, rhythms, attention and character choices from automatic normalization.

**Important difference / exceptions:** The expanded version explicitly distinguishes error from meaningful irregularity; it does not prohibit useful editing. Preserve those qualifications.

**Proposed cross-reference, NOT a merge:** One primary principle under different titles.

**Risk:** Low if the legitimate-revision exceptions remain.

### AR-09 — Distinct

**Short index:** “Homogenization resistance” (SRC-003 lines 2350–2351).  
**Expanded manual:** “Intentional omission & unresolved space” (SRC-003 lines 18915–19007).

**Source-supported comparison:** Short entry resists revising all prose toward generic smoothness and conventional polish. Expanded entry protects deliberate unknowns, unclosed questions and interpretive space.

**Important difference / exceptions:** Both defend artistic identity but operate on different dimensions: stylistic originality versus narrative/interpretive closure. Expanded text directly preserves homogenization concerns in AR-08 and the standalone Anti-Homogenization Test around lines 19604–19653.

**Proposed cross-reference, NOT a merge:** Short homogenization → expanded AR-08 plus Anti-Homogenization Test; expanded intentional omission → NI-07 Productive omission and AR-10 identity also relate.

**Risk:** HIGH: the short lens would cease checking over-polishing if equated to unresolved narrative space.

### AR-10 — Overlapping, not identical

**Short index:** “Artistic coherence” (SRC-003 lines 2353–2354).  
**Expanded manual:** “Work-specific artistic identity” (SRC-003 lines 19008–19173).

**Source-supported comparison:** Short entry asks style, imagery, structure, characterization and theme to feel native to the same novel. Expanded entry asks whether the novel possesses a distinctive governing sensibility shaped by selection, refusal and priorities.

**Important difference / exceptions:** Coherence is necessary to many forms of identity but not sufficient to prove distinction; a coherent work can still be generic. The expanded lens accommodates deliberately accessible and conventional styles.

**Proposed cross-reference, NOT a merge:** Short artistic coherence → expanded AR-07 aesthetic coherence and AR-10 identity; expanded AR-10 functions as an umbrella concern.

**Risk:** Medium: do not equate polish/consistency with individuality.

## Proposed options for author review (none approved)

**Option A — Keep the short index canonical and treat expanded text as commentary:** simple backwards references, but incorrectly attaches some later expanded AR manuals to unrelated short IDs unless relabeled or split.

**Option B — Keep the expanded manual canonical and preserve the short index as historical:** consistent with later detailed text, but risks dropping original intended moral complexity, work-specific obsessions and general anti-homogenization rules, and would require source-traceable preservation.

**Option C — Preserve both source versions and create an owner-approved reconciled taxonomy:** retain each original labeled variant, identify overlap and concept splits, and only assign stable canonical IDs after review. This is the *researcher's recommendation for evaluation*, because it minimizes conceptual loss; it is **not a final taxonomy, migration schema or software architecture decision**.

**Option D — Keep both lists permanently as separate editions:** maximum fidelity, but potential duplicate rule effects and a more complicated writer-facing experience. Only appropriate with deliberate version semantics.
